SPEAKER NOTES - CME segmentation deck
=====================================

P01  What we are looking at
  An OCT scan is a cross-section through the retina, like a slice
  through a cake. In edema, fluid collects in pockets - the dark
  holes. A clinician currently outlines those by hand.

P02  How it works
  Five steps, no machine learning, no training data. Clean the
  speckle, locate the retina, find the dark pockets inside it,
  keep the ones that look like fluid.

P03  Results
  Green is the specialist. Red is our code, fully automatic.
  Six different patients, one setting used for all of them.

P04  How good is that, really?
  Over ALL 78 expert-annotated scans we overlap the
  specialist 43% of the time. On the larger cysts, 61%.
  KEY POINT - the ceiling is not 100%. Two trained eye
  specialists tracing the SAME scans agree with each other
  only 58% of the time overall, and 74% on the larger cysts.
  So we reach about 74% of what two humans achieve overall,
  and about 82% of it on the clinically significant lesions.
  Both numbers measured on the same scans - a like-for-like
  comparison, not our best subset against their worst.

P05  Clinical output
  Neither reference paper goes past the outline. We convert it to
  fluid area, cyst count, size and a severity grade.

P06  Speed
  0.42 s per scan on a normal laptop, no GPU.

P07  Why four directions
  The operator sweeps the image like a wave. Four sweep directions
  find the most fluid AND run fastest. Our data reproduces the
  choice the original authors made.

IF ASKED - honest answers, all backed by docs/FINDINGS.md
  "Did you reproduce the paper exactly?"  We implemented it and it
     did not transfer to this dataset unchanged; our modifications
     were needed. The measurements are in the report.
  "Is it overfitted?"  No - leave-one-subject-out validation, where
     settings are tuned on nine patients and tested on the tenth,
     loses only ~0.015 overlap.
  "Any machine learning?"  None anywhere.
